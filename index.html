STILL A WORK IN PROGRESS

I thought I had a solution for organizing cables in the lab where I work. I made it — and it didn't work the way I'd imagined.

I redesigned it. Then again. And again. I still haven't reached a final version; I keep finding things to improve.

When I explained it to a colleague, he offered another way to look at the problem — an angle I hadn't considered.

His perspective changed how I imagined the solution could look and work. I felt curious: suddenly there were new directions to test and discover.

What might another pair of eyes notice? ↘

SOMETHING I MADE

During my army service, the room's air conditioner had no remote. Its power button was so high that switching it on meant balancing on a chair.

I made a wired control using an aircraft-style switch housed in a small coffee tin. Suddenly, turning it on didn't mean climbing.

An everyday irritation became something to experiment with.

What everyday thing would you change? ↘

AN IDEA WE EXPLORED

A friend wanted to make money. I wanted to understand why — and what we could begin with the tools already available to us.

We found an idea that interested us both: software to help electricians create wiring plans from building images. It was an idea for something useful, beyond the financial goal.

My first move is usually a question: why, how, or what?

What could you begin with what you have? ↘
